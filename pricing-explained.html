Blender render farm pricing

GPU Render Studio measures your actual Blender project before the full job begins. You review the expected GPU runtime, suitable plan and price before approving the render.

How pricing works

  1. Upload a finished Blender project.
  2. A real GPU precheck measures render behaviour and estimates total GPU time.
  3. The system recommends the smallest suitable render plan.
  4. You review the measured estimate and price.
  5. The paid full render starts only after approval.

For normal plans, the render charge is the greater of the plan minimum or actual GPU render time multiplied by the plan's hourly rate.

Why we measure the scene instead of quoting by file size

Blender render cost depends on the work performed by the renderer, not simply the size of the .blend file. Sampling, geometry, textures, lighting, volumetrics, compositing, effects, resolution and frame count can all change runtime substantially.

A measured precheck gives you project-specific information before you commit. Read how the workflow works or see our measured RTX 5090 Cycles benchmark.

Is a render farm worth it for every project?

No. If your scene already renders quickly on your own GPU, local rendering may make more sense. A render service becomes more useful when local rendering ties up the workstation, a deadline matters, or buying high-end hardware for occasional workloads would be inefficient.

The precheck is designed to help you make that decision with the actual project rather than a generic estimate.
